# Task 4 – Final Investment Analysis Report
## Tata Motors Group (FY 2024–25 historical analysis)

**Prepared for:** Academic financial-analysis assignment  
**Company / reporting basis:** Former Tata Motors Limited consolidated group, FY ended 31 March 2025  
**Currency:** Indian rupees; ₹ crore unless stated otherwise  
**Purpose:** Educational company and security-analysis exercise only. This report is not a recommendation to buy, hold or sell any security.

## 1. Executive summary

Tata Motors' FY25 performance showed a mixed but financially resilient picture. Consolidated revenue from operations increased modestly to ₹4,39,695 crore from ₹4,34,016 crore in FY24. The underlying EBITDA margin declined from 14.1% to 13.1%, while profit for the year declined to ₹28,149 crore from ₹31,807 crore. Automotive free cash flow remained strongly positive at ₹22,348 crore, and the automotive business reported net cash of ₹1,018 crore at 31 March 2025.

The historical investment case therefore combined strong cash generation, global scale and established positions in commercial vehicles, passenger vehicles and Jaguar Land Rover (JLR), against margin pressure, cyclical demand, global operating exposure and the investment needs of the transition to electric and lower-emission mobility.

**Important corporate-structure qualification:** Tata Motors' composite demerger became effective on 1 October 2025. The former group was separated into distinct commercial-vehicle and passenger-vehicle businesses. FY25 consolidated figures in this report describe the pre-demerger group and must not be treated as the current standalone financials of either successor listed company.

## 2. Business overview

The former Tata Motors group operated across:
- **Commercial vehicles (CV):** trucks, buses, pickups and last-mile mobility.
- **Passenger vehicles (PV):** cars and SUVs, including electric vehicles.
- **Jaguar Land Rover (JLR):** global premium and luxury vehicles.
- **Related mobility and automotive activities:** financing, services, components and associated investments.

This portfolio gave the group exposure to multiple customer segments and geographies. It also meant performance depended on different demand cycles, currencies, regulations and competitive conditions.

## 3. Financial performance review

| Indicator | FY25 | FY24 | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Revenue from operations | ₹4,39,695 cr | ₹4,34,016 cr | Increased about 1.3%; growth was modest |
| Underlying EBITDA margin | 13.1% | 14.1% | Declined 100 basis points; profitability pressure |
| Profit for the year | ₹28,149 cr | ₹31,807 cr | Lower year-on-year |
| Automotive free cash flow | ₹22,348 cr | ₹26,925 cr | Strong positive cash generation, but lower than FY24 |
| Net automotive debt/(cash) | ₹(1,018) cr | ₹16,022 cr debt | Automotive business moved to net-cash position |
| Basic EPS | ₹78.80 | ₹81.95 | Lower year-on-year |

The FY25 annual report also reported PBT before exceptional items of ₹34,330 crore, compared with ₹29,368 crore in FY24. Different profit measures can move in different directions because of tax, exceptional items, discontinued operations and other accounting effects; they should not be treated as interchangeable.

### Financial interpretation

1. Revenue growth was positive but limited, suggesting the group was not relying on rapid top-line expansion in FY25.
2. The EBITDA margin reduction indicates that cost, product mix, pricing or operating conditions pressured profitability.
3. Positive automotive free cash flow and the move to net automotive cash were important balance-sheet strengths.
4. Lower reported profit and lower free cash flow versus FY24 show that strong liquidity does not eliminate earnings volatility.

## 4. Investment thesis – historical FY25 perspective

### Potential strengths

- **Established market positions:** The group had established scale and brands in Indian commercial vehicles and passenger vehicles, alongside JLR's global premium presence.
- **Cash generation:** Automotive free cash flow of ₹22,348 crore provided internal funding capacity for investment and balance-sheet resilience.
- **Improved automotive net position:** Net automotive cash of ₹1,018 crore at FY25 year-end reduced financial-risk pressure compared with FY24's net automotive debt.
- **Multiple growth avenues:** Product launches, premiumisation, exports, electric mobility, connected-vehicle services and fleet solutions offered potential avenues for future growth.
- **Strategic focus after demerger:** Separate CV and PV-focused entities may allow more tailored capital allocation, management attention and business strategies. The benefits depend on execution and should not be assumed in advance.

### What would support a stronger outlook

A more constructive outlook would require evidence of sustained revenue growth, recovery or stability in operating margins, disciplined capital expenditure, continued positive free cash flow, healthy demand across major markets and successful execution of the post-demerger strategy.

## 6. Key risks

| Risk | Why it matters | What an analyst should monitor |
|---|---|---|
| Margin and cost pressure | Lower margins can reduce earnings even when revenue grows | EBITDA/EBIT margins, material costs, pricing and product mix |
| Economic and vehicle-demand cycles | CV demand is linked to freight, infrastructure, fleet utilisation and economic activity; PV demand is sensitive to consumer confidence and financing costs | Industry volumes, order books, dealer inventory and utilisation |
| JLR geographic and currency exposure | Global sales expose the business to exchange rates, tariffs, geopolitical developments and regional demand shifts | Regional sales, currency movements, tariffs and JLR profitability |
| Competition and technology transition | EVs, software, safety and emissions technologies require investment; competitors may gain share | Product launches, EV adoption, market share, warranty and R&D costs |
| Capital expenditure and execution | New platforms, factories and technology investments may not earn expected returns | Capex, project milestones, return on capital and free cash flow |
| Regulation and compliance | Emissions, safety, trade and local-content rules can increase costs or constrain products | Regulatory announcements, recall data and compliance spending |
| Demerger and transition risk | Separate entities need clear governance, systems, funding and capital allocation | Standalone reporting, related-party arrangements, costs and strategy delivery |

These are analytical risk categories, not predictions that any particular event will occur.

## 7. Overall assessment

On the FY25 historical evidence, the former Tata Motors group had meaningful strengths in scale, brand portfolio, positive automotive free cash flow and a net-cash automotive position. The main caution was that revenue growth was modest, EBITDA margin contracted and reported profit and free cash flow were lower than FY24.

The demerger is a major change in the analytical perimeter. It may help each business pursue a more focused strategy, but it also means the old consolidated financial picture is no longer sufficient for a current investment decision. Any present-day analysis should be rebuilt separately for the successor CV and PV entities using their latest audited and interim disclosures.

**Conclusion:** FY25 presented a financially resilient but not uniformly improving business profile. The evidence supports a balanced watch-list view rather than a categorical bullish or bearish conclusion. The outcome depends on margin recovery, cash conversion, demand conditions, capital discipline and post-demerger execution.
